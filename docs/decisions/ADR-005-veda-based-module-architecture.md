# ADR-005: Veda-Based Governance Pillar Architecture & Terminology
**Status:** Approved  
**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Scope:** Domain Taxonomy & Modular Monolith Partitioning

## Context
Initial scaffolding used generic placeholder terminology such as "Pillar 1", "Pillar 2", "Pillar 3", which lacked institutional meaning and strategic cohesion with the vision of Chhatrapati Shahu Ji Maharaj University (CSJMU).

## Decision
The 4 foundational R&D governance pillars of VYASA are officially anchored in **Veda-inspired domain taxonomy**:

1. **RIG VEDA:** Research & Knowledge Creation  
   *Domain:* Foundational research discovery, doctoral thesis preparation, synopsis progression, publication repositories.
2. **YAJUR VEDA:** Research Administration & Incentives  
   *Domain:* Institutional project sanctioning, funding compliance, research grant disbursement, ethics reviews.
3. **SAMA VEDA:** Research Recognition & Communication  
   *Domain:* Faculty citation telemetry, scholar recognition, symposium management, public knowledge dissemination.
4. **ATHARVA VEDA:** Grievance Redressal & Institutional Well-Being  
   *Domain:* Institutional grievance redressal, accountable hierarchical triage, special committee deliberative voting, cryptographic dossier assembly (NIVARAN-AI subsystem).

Rig Veda, Yajur Veda, and Sama Veda are designated as future development domains. In Phase 2, only their structural module boundaries, folder layouts, and contracts are established without fabricating premature business logic. NIVARAN-AI is the existing functional implementation and is categorized under Atharva Veda.

## Alternatives Considered
- **Generic Numbering ("Pillar 1..4"):** Abandoned for lack of domain identity and semantic clarity.
- **English Functional Names ("Research", "Finance", "Awards", "Complaints"):** Replaced by the approved university Veda governance taxonomy.

## Consequences
- **Positive:** Clear, culturally grounded, long-term domain boundaries with high conceptual resonance for CSJMU leadership.
- **Negative:** Requires ongoing education for new engineering team members regarding domain mappings.
